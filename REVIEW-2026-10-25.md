### Prompt 87: Analyse the impact of the feudal system on medieval Europe. Write ONE TEEAL paragraph. Choose the area you think it shaped most (power, land, daily life or warfare), give at least two specific pieces of evidence and reach a judgement.

- **T** The feudal system shaped medieval Europe most deeply through power, because it decided who could command whom and turned land into the source of all authority.
- **E** After 1066 William the Conqueror claimed all of England as his own and granted it in fiefs to about 180 barons, who in turn granted land to knights, so that by the Domesday survey of 1086 every acre could be traced back to the king.
- **A** Because each grant came with an oath of loyalty and a duty to supply soldiers, the king could raise an army without paying one, and a lord’s wealth, status and ability to fight all came from the land he held rather than from money or office.
- **E** The same system reached down to the manor, where peasants farmed strips of the lord’s land and owed him labour, rents and a share of their harvest, and could not leave without permission.
- **A** This meant the great majority of people lived under the direct authority of a local lord rather than a distant king, and their daily lives, from the court they were judged in to the mill they used, were controlled by him.
- **L** Although feudalism also shaped warfare and the economy, its deepest impact was on power, because it bound every level of society into a chain of obligation that lasted until the Black Death loosened it in the fourteenth century.

*Marks shown: A 3  K 2  S 1  W 2  =  8 / 8.* *Why:* The question asks for an analysis, and the contention chooses one area (power) and says why it matters most. Evidence is specific and dated (1066, 180 barons, 1086). Each piece of evidence is followed by a because or this meant sentence. The link names the other areas, ranks them beneath the chosen one and marks where the system began to change.

### Prompt 88: Write ONE TEEAL paragraph in response to the following. Evaluate how the Catholic Church maintained its influence over medieval society. Choose the type of power you think mattered most (political, social, economic or cultural), support it with at least two specific examples and explain why it was more important than the others.

- **T** The Catholic Church maintained its influence over medieval society mainly through social power, because it was present at every stage of ordinary life in a way no king could match.
- **E** Every person was baptised, married and buried by the Church, and the year itself was organised around holy days and festivals, so religion shaped time as well as belief.
- **EA** Because most people could not read, the Church taught through what they could see, such as doom paintings of heaven and hell on church walls, which made the threat of damnation vivid and kept people obedient to its rules.
- **E** The Church also held economic and political power. The tithe took one tenth of every harvest, and the Pope could excommunicate a king, as Innocent III did to King John in 1209.
- **A** However, these powers depended on the social hold underneath them, since a tithe is only paid and excommunication is only feared by people who already believe the Church controls their path to heaven.
- **L** This is why social power mattered most, and why Church authority only weakened when the Black Death shook that everyday belief.

*Marks shown: A 3  K 2  S 1  W 2  =  8 / 8.* *Why:* The question asks for a choice, and the paragraph makes one in the first sentence. The other forms of power are named but then ranked beneath the chosen one, which is evaluation rather than a list. Evidence is specific (tithe, King John, doom paintings).

### Prompt 89: Analyse the significance of sakoku for Japan. Write ONE TEEAL paragraph. Consider what the policy was meant to achieve, what it changed inside Japan and whether its effects lasted.

- **T** Sakoku was significant for Japan mainly because it bought the Tokugawa two centuries of stability at the price of falling behind the rest of the world.
- **E** Between 1633 and 1639 the shogunate issued edicts that banned Christianity, forbade Japanese people from leaving the country and expelled the Portuguese, leaving only the Dutch and Chinese to trade through the small island of Dejima at Nagasaki.
- **A** Because foreign ideas, weapons and religion could no longer reach the daimyo, the shoguns removed the outside support a rebel lord might have used, and Japan avoided the colonisation that European powers imposed elsewhere in Asia.
- **E** Inside the country, peace allowed Edo to grow to around a million people by 1700 and a rich merchant culture of theatre and woodblock prints to develop.
- **A** However, because Japan did not share in the industrial and military changes happening in Europe, when Commodore Perry’s steamships arrived in 1853 the shogunate had no way to resist and was forced to sign unequal treaties.
- **L** Sakoku was therefore significant in two directions: it was the foundation of the long Tokugawa peace, and the reason that peace ended so suddenly when the policy could no longer be enforced.

*Marks shown: A 3  K 2  S 1  W 2  =  8 / 8.* *Why:* Significance is argued, not asserted: the contention weighs a benefit against a cost. Evidence is dated and specific (1633 to 1639, Dejima, a million people, 1853). The paragraph covers cause and effect in both directions and the link reaches a judgement that answers the question rather than restating it.

**Q156** (Tokugawa Shogunate, mc) Edo grew from a fishing village in 1600 to a city of about a million people by 1700. Which Tokugawa policy did most to cause this growth?

  *Explanation:* Alternate attendance filled Edo with daimyo households, their samurai and the merchants and craftspeople who served them. The constant demand for goods and services made Edo one of the largest cities in the world.

**Q157** (Tokugawa Shogunate, mc) Kabuki theatre, ukiyo-e woodblock prints and popular novels all flourished in Edo in the 1700s. Who was the main audience for this new culture?
  
  **Townspeople (chonin), especially merchants with money to spend** / The emperor's court in Kyoto / Farmers in the countryside / Buddhist monks
  
  *Explanation:* Merchants ranked lowest officially but grew wealthy under two centuries of peace. Their money paid for a lively urban culture of theatres, prints, teahouses and fiction aimed at ordinary townspeople.

**Q158** (Tokugawa Shogunate, ms) Select ALL the ways the long Tokugawa peace changed life in Japan's cities.
  
  **Merchants grew wealthy and influential despite their low official rank** / **Samurai increasingly worked as officials and administrators rather than fighting** / **Popular entertainment such as kabuki and woodblock prints developed** / Castles were abandoned and towns had no walls or defences / Christianity became the main religion of townspeople
  
  *Explanation:* Peace turned warriors into bureaucrats and let a money economy grow. Castle towns remained the centres of daimyo power, and Christianity was banned, so the last two are wrong.

**Q159** (Tokugawa Shogunate, mc) Travellers on the Tokaido road between Edo and Kyoto often crossed rivers and inlets by ferry or were carried across by porters rather than using bridges. Why?
  
  **The shogunate limited bridges on major rivers so armies could not move quickly against Edo, and Japan's mountains and rivers made roads hard to build** / Bridges were forbidden by Buddhist teaching / Japanese builders did not know how to build bridges / The rivers were too shallow for bridges
  
  *Explanation:* Japan's geography of mountains, fast rivers and coastal inlets made overland travel slow, and the Tokugawa deliberately left some rivers unbridged and set checkpoints to control movement toward Edo.

**Q160** (Tokugawa Shogunate, mc) Post towns with inns, porters and horses grew up along the Tokaido and other highways during the Edo period. What created the demand for them?
  
  **Daimyo processions under sankin kotai, plus pilgrims, merchants and officials travelling the roads** / Foreign tourists arriving under sakoku / Farmers moving to Edo to become samurai / Christian missionaries travelling between churches
  
  *Explanation:* Alternate attendance sent thousands of people up and down the highways every year. Post towns and ferry crossings grew to serve them, and the roads became busy with pilgrims and traders as well.

**Q161** (Chronology, mc) Put these periods of Japanese history in the correct order, earliest first.
  
  **Heian, Kamakura, Muromachi, Edo** / Kamakura, Heian, Edo, Muromachi / Muromachi, Kamakura, Heian, Edo / Heian, Muromachi, Kamakura, Edo
  
  *Explanation:* Heian (794 to 1185) was the age of the imperial court. Kamakura (1185 to 1333) began with the first shogun. Muromachi (1336 to 1573) was the age of the Ashikaga shoguns. Edo (1600 to 1868) was the Tokugawa period.

**Q162** (Chronology, mc) A timeline shows the Onin War in 1467 and the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600. Which century does each belong to?
  
  **15th century and 17th century** / 14th century and 16th century / 15th century and 16th century / 16th century and 17th century
  
  *Explanation:* To find the century, add one to the hundreds digit. 1467 is in the 1400s, so the 15th century. 1600 is the first year of the 17th century, since the 16th century ran from 1501 to 1600.

**Q163** (Chronology, mc) Which of these happened during the Kamakura period (1185 to 1333)?
  
  **The Mongol invasions of Japan** / Perry's arrival in Edo Bay / The sakoku edicts / The Onin War
  
  *Explanation:* The Mongols attacked in 1274 and 1281, under the Kamakura shogunate. The Onin War was 1467 (Muromachi), sakoku the 1630s and Perry 1853 (both Edo).

**Q164** (Source analysis, mc) A history book shows a colour woodblock print of a crowded theatre, made in Japan in 1765. How should this source be described?
  
  **A primary source: a visual (artwork) created at the time it depicts** / A secondary source, because it is printed in a book / A primary source: a written document / A secondary source, because the artist was not a historian
  
  *Explanation:* Type of source means the form it takes: a print, painting, letter, chronicle, map and so on. Origin means who made it, where and when. A print made in 1765 of Edo-period life is a primary visual source regardless of where it is reproduced today.

**Q165** (Source analysis, mc) A caption reads: 'Illuminated manuscript produced in Genoa, Italy, c. 1290, artist unknown.' Which of these can you state from the caption alone?
  
  **Where and roughly when it was made** / Why the artist made it / Whether the scene really happened / Who paid for it
  
  *Explanation:* Captions usually give origin: place, date and (if known) creator. Purpose, accuracy and patron have to be inferred from the content and your own knowledge, and you should signal that with words like 'suggests' or 'implies'.

**Q166** (Source analysis, short) A medieval illumination shows two knights jousting while a crowd watches from castle walls. Write one sentence that INFERS something about knights from the picture, using the word 'suggests'.
  
  *Explanation:* A good inference links a detail to a conclusion: 'The watching crowd suggests tournaments were public displays of skill that built a knight's reputation.' Weak answers only describe what is in the picture.

### s1. Explain one reason William the Conqueror built castles across England after 1066. (2 marks, Medieval Europe, Explain one)

**2/2** One reason was to control a hostile population. William had conquered England with a small Norman army, so he built motte-and-bailey castles in towns such as York and Nottingham. Because a castle let a few dozen soldiers dominate the surrounding countryside and gave them a safe base if the English rebelled, he could hold the kingdom without a large army.  
*Why:* Identifies a reason (control), gives specific evidence (small army, York, Nottingham) and links with a because sentence to how castles solved the problem.

**1/2** William built castles to protect himself. Castles were strong and had high walls and a moat, so the Normans were safe inside them.  
*Why:* Identifies protection, which earns 1. The rest describes what a castle was like rather than explaining why building many of them across England helped William rule.

### s2. Describe three obligations a serf owed to the lord of the manor. (3 marks, Medieval Europe, Describe three)

**3/3** First, serfs had to work the lord’s own land, the demesne, for several days each week before working their own strips. Second, they paid the lord in kind, handing over a share of their harvest and animals, and paid to use the lord’s mill and oven. Third, they could not leave the manor, marry or send a son to the Church without the lord’s permission, usually paid for with a fee.  
*Why:* Three separate obligations, each with a specific detail. Labour, payment and restriction on movement are different kinds of duty, so none repeats another.

**1/3** Serfs had to work for the lord. They worked on his land and did farming for him and also did other jobs he told them to do.  
*Why:* Working the lord’s land is one obligation, so 1 mark. The other sentences repeat the same point in different words instead of naming a second and third obligation.

### s3. Explain two reasons why people joined the First Crusade in 1096. (4 marks, Medieval Europe, Explain two)

**4/4** One reason was religious. At Clermont in 1095 Pope Urban II promised that anyone who died on crusade would have their sins forgiven. Because medieval people feared hell and believed the Pope spoke for God, many saw the journey as a way to secure their place in heaven.
A second reason was land and wealth. Younger sons of nobles inherited nothing under the rule that the eldest son took the whole estate. Because the East was described as rich and the Pope said conquered land could be kept, crusading offered them a chance to gain property they could never have at home.  
*Why:* Two clearly different reasons, each identified in its first sentence, supported with specific evidence (Clermont 1095, forgiveness of sins, inheritance rules) and linked with a because sentence. Two marks for each.

**2/4** People joined the Crusades for religious reasons. The Pope told them to go and fight for Jerusalem and said it was God’s will. They also wanted to get to heaven and be forgiven for their sins. Some people went because they were very religious and wanted to see the Holy Land.  
*Why:* Only one reason, religion, is given, in four different ways. It is identified and explained, so it earns both marks for that reason, but there is no second reason, so the other two marks are lost.

### s4. Define the term excommunication and give one example of a ruler it was used against. (2 marks, Medieval Europe, Define and example)

**2/2** Excommunication was a punishment in which the Church expelled a person from the Christian community, cutting them off from the sacraments so that they could not be married, confessed or buried by a priest and were believed to be bound for hell. Pope Innocent III excommunicated King John of England in 1209 during their dispute over the Archbishop of Canterbury.  
*Why:* The definition says what it was and why it mattered. The example is a named ruler with a date and the reason.

**1/2** Excommunication is when someone gets kicked out of the Church by the Pope. It was used against kings.  
*Why:* The definition is basically right, so 1 mark. The example is too vague to earn the second mark; it needs a named ruler.

### s5. Explain why the Peasants’ Revolt of 1381 is considered a turning point in medieval England. (3 marks, Medieval Europe, Turning point)

**3/3** The Peasants’ Revolt is considered a turning point because it showed that the old feudal order could no longer be enforced. In 1381 thousands of peasants marched on London, killed the Archbishop of Canterbury and demanded an end to serfdom. Although the revolt was crushed and its leader Wat Tyler was killed, lords found they could not make the hated poll tax or the old labour services stick, and over the following decades serfdom in England quietly disappeared, so the direction of change ran towards free, waged labour.  
*Why:* Climbs the ladder. What happened, what changed, who was affected and for how long. Admits the short-term failure and still shows the long-term change, with the word because in the first sentence.

**1/3** The Peasants’ Revolt was in 1381. The peasants were angry about the poll tax and marched to London. Wat Tyler was the leader and he was killed by the king’s men. It was a very important event.  
*Why:* Accurate description of what happened, so 1 mark. Nothing on what changed afterwards or for how long, which is what the turning-point marks are for.

### s6. Compare trial by ordeal and trial by jury in medieval England. Include at least one similarity and one difference. (4 marks, Medieval Europe, Compare)

**4/4** Both trial by ordeal and trial by jury were ways of deciding guilt when there was no police force and little written evidence, for example both were used for serious crimes such as theft and both were run under the authority of the king’s law. However, trial by ordeal left the verdict to God, through tests such as carrying a hot iron and seeing whether the wound healed, whereas trial by jury left it to twelve local men who judged the accused on what they knew of them, which meant that after the Church banned priests from ordeals in 1215, juries became the normal way of deciding cases in England.  
*Why:* Similarity with evidence, difference with evidence, and a consequence in the which-meant clause. The frame sentence earns all four marks.

**2/4** Trial by ordeal was when you had to carry a hot iron or be thrown in water to see if you were guilty. Trial by jury was when people decided if you were guilty. They were both used in medieval times.  
*Why:* A difference is identified with some evidence, so 2 marks. Both being medieval is not a real similarity, and there is no consequence.

### s7. Explain one way the Catholic Church gained wealth in medieval Europe. (2 marks, Medieval Europe, Explain one)

**2/2** One way was the tithe. Every villager had to give one tenth of what they produced to the Church each year, stored in the tithe barn. Because almost everyone in Europe was a Christian and the tithe was collected whether the harvest was good or bad, it gave the Church a steady income from the whole population that no king could match.  
*Why:* Identifies the tithe, gives the detail (one tenth, tithe barn) and links to why it made the Church wealthy.

**1/2** The Church got money from the tithe, which was a tax people had to pay to the Church.  
*Why:* Identifies the tithe correctly, so 1 mark. No because sentence explaining why it made the Church so wealthy.

### s8. Explain how the Domesday Book helped William the Conqueror control England. (3 marks, Medieval Europe, Explain how)

**3/3** The Domesday Book was a survey ordered in 1085 that recorded who held every piece of land in England, what it was worth and what it had been worth before 1066. Because William now knew exactly what each lord held, he could tax them accurately and could settle disputes over land in his own favour. It also fixed in writing that all land was ultimately the king’s, which strengthened the feudal chain of loyalty that his rule depended on.  
*Why:* Says what it was, then two because or which links to control: taxation and ownership. Specific date and detail.

**1/3** The Domesday Book was a big book that listed everything in England, like how many pigs and cows people had. It took a long time to make and was finished in 1086.  
*Why:* Accurate description, so 1 mark. The question asks how it helped William control England, and there is no link to control.

### s9. Explain one reason the Tokugawa shoguns introduced sankin kotai. (2 marks, Shogunate Japan, Explain one)

**2/2** One reason was to weaken the daimyo financially. Under sankin kotai every daimyo had to keep a residence in Edo and travel there with a large procession every second year. Because the cost of two households and constant travel ate up much of a domain’s income, daimyo could not afford to build up armies that might challenge the shogun.  
*Why:* Identifies one reason, gives the detail of the policy and links the cost to the shogun’s security with a because sentence.

**1/2** The shoguns introduced sankin kotai so they could control the daimyo. The daimyo had to go to Edo every second year and leave their family there.  
*Why:* Identifies control and describes the policy, so 1 mark. It never says how the policy produced control, which is the second mark.

### s10. Describe three expectations placed on samurai under the code of bushido. (3 marks, Shogunate Japan, Describe three)

**3/3** First, absolute loyalty to their lord, which was expected to come before loyalty to family or even their own life. Second, courage and skill in combat, including training with the sword and bow from childhood and a willingness to die in battle rather than retreat. Third, honour, so that a samurai who had failed or disgraced his lord was expected to take his own life through seppuku rather than live with the shame.  
*Why:* Three distinct expectations, each with a concrete detail. Loyalty, courage and honour are separate ideas, so none repeats another.

**1/3** Samurai had to be loyal to their lord. They had to do everything the lord said and follow him into battle and never betray him.  
*Why:* Loyalty is one expectation, so 1 mark. The other sentences are more loyalty rather than a second and third expectation.

### s11. Explain two ways the Meiji government changed the position of the samurai after 1868. (4 marks, Shogunate Japan, Explain two)

**4/4** One change was the end of their military role. In 1873 the government introduced conscription, so that ordinary men of all classes were drafted into a Western-style army. Because the state no longer needed a hereditary warrior class, the samurai lost the thing that had defined them for seven centuries.
A second change was the loss of their privileges. The government stopped paying rice stipends, replacing them with bonds in 1876, and in the same year banned the wearing of swords in public. Because stipends and swords had marked samurai out from everyone else, these laws removed both their income and their visible status, which led some to rebel in the Satsuma Rebellion of 1877.  
*Why:* Two separate changes, each identified, evidenced with a date and explained with a because sentence. The second adds a consequence, which is not required but shows understanding.

**2/4** The samurai were not allowed to carry swords any more. They also lost their stipends and had to find other jobs. Many samurai became unhappy and some rebelled against the government in 1877.  
*Why:* Two changes are identified, so 1 mark each, but neither is explained with a because sentence about why the change mattered to the samurai’s position, so the two explanation marks are lost.

### s12. Define the term shogun and give one example of a shogun. (2 marks, Shogunate Japan, Define and example)

**2/2** A shogun was the military ruler of Japan, appointed in theory by the emperor but in practice holding real political power while the emperor remained a ceremonial figure. Tokugawa Ieyasu became shogun in 1603 after his victory at Sekigahara and founded a dynasty that ruled until 1868.  
*Why:* The definition covers both the military role and the relationship to the emperor. The example is a named shogun with a date.

**1/2** A shogun was the leader of Japan who was like a king. An example is the Tokugawa.  
*Why:* The definition is roughly right but misses that the emperor still existed above the shogun; the Tokugawa were a family, not a shogun. Together worth 1.

### s13. Explain why the Meiji Restoration of 1868 is considered a turning point in Japanese history. (3 marks, Shogunate Japan, Turning point)

**3/3** The Meiji Restoration is considered a turning point because it ended nearly seven centuries of rule by shoguns and set Japan on a completely different course. In 1868 the last Tokugawa shogun resigned and power was restored to the fifteen-year-old Emperor Meiji, whose government then abolished the domains, ended the samurai class and built railways, factories and a conscript army. Within forty years Japan had become an industrial power able to defeat Russia in 1905, so the direction of its history changed from isolation and feudalism to modernisation and empire.  
*Why:* What happened, what changed, who was affected and for how long, with the word because in the first sentence and a dated consequence at the end.

**1/3** The Meiji Restoration happened in 1868. The emperor got his power back from the shogun. Meiji means enlightened rule and the emperor was only fifteen. It changed Japan a lot.  
*Why:* Accurate description, so 1 mark. Changed Japan a lot is a claim, not an explanation of what changed or for how long.

### s14. Identify one thing that changed and one thing that stayed the same in Japan after the Meiji Restoration. Explain each. (4 marks, Shogunate Japan, Continuity and change)

**4/4** One change was the social hierarchy. The four-class system was abolished in the early 1870s and the samurai lost their stipends and swords, which meant that for the first time a merchant’s son and a samurai’s son were legally equal and could both be conscripted or go to the new state schools.
One continuity was rule by a small elite. Although the emperor was restored, real decisions were made by a group of former samurai from Satsuma and Choshu, and the 1889 constitution gave the vote to only about one per cent of the population, which meant that ordinary Japanese people had little more say in government than under the shoguns.  
*Why:* Change and continuity are each identified, evidenced and explained with a which-meant clause. The continuity is a genuine one, not just something that was slow to change.

**2/4** A change was that Japan got railways and factories and became modern. Something that stayed the same was that Japan still had an emperor. The emperor had been there before and he was still there after.  
*Why:* A change and a continuity are identified, so 2 marks. Neither is explained; saying the emperor was still there does not explain what that meant for how Japan was governed.

### s15. Explain two reasons the Tokugawa shogunate was already weakening before Perry arrived in 1853. (4 marks, Shogunate Japan, Explain two)

**4/4** One reason was economic. Under two centuries of peace, merchants grew rich while samurai, paid in fixed rice stipends, fell into debt to them. Because the people at the top of the official hierarchy were now dependent on those at the bottom, the social order the shogunate was built on no longer matched reality.
A second reason was the shogunate’s failure to deal with hardship. Famines in the 1830s led to peasant uprisings and even a revolt by a former official, Oshio Heihachiro, in Osaka in 1837. Because the shogunate could neither prevent the famines nor stop the unrest, people began to doubt that it was capable of governing, which made it easier to challenge after 1853.  
*Why:* Two distinct reasons, economic and political, each with specific evidence and a because sentence. The second links forward to 1853 without making Perry the subject.

**2/4** The shogunate was weakening because the merchants were getting rich and the samurai were poor. Also there were famines in the 1830s and people were unhappy and had riots. So the shogunate was already weak when Perry came.  
*Why:* Two reasons are identified, 1 mark each. Neither is explained; the answer never says why rich merchants or famines weakened the shogunate rather than just happening.
