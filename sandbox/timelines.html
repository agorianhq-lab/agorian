Two worlds, one timeline

Medieval Europe on the left, Japan on the right, on the same scale. Click an event for a line of detail; click again or press Escape to close it. Dotted gold links mark moments when the two units rhyme. The scale is stretched between 1050 and 1500, where most of both units sits.

Which dates to learn. The starred years (★) are the ones worth writing in a Section B or C answer, because a date is the quickest way to make a point specific. The rest are here so you can see where things sit. And in any exam: every source comes with dates, in its caption or on the source itself. Use them. A date you can see on the paper is a free piece of evidence for a written answer, and a check on the dates you remember.

Ashikaga shogunateThe Muromachi period. Weak shoguns, strong daimyo.

Dates are the ones used in class. Where historians give a range (the Genpei War, the Mongol invasions, the Black Death), the first year is shown. The Edo period is dated from 1600 (Sekigahara) or 1603 (Ieyasu named shogun); both are used.
