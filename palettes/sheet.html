B · Indigo and persimmon. Edo dye colours

Built from ai indigo and kaki persimmon, the two dyes of Edo-period cloth, with a cooler near-white ground and a sharper red. Crisper and more modern than A while keeping the Japan unit at its heart. Europe shifts to a wine red, Japan to a deep indigo, so the two units sit further apart.

C · Lapis and vermilion. Illuminated manuscript

From the pigments of medieval manuscripts, ultramarine from lapis lazuli, vermilion, and gold leaf, on a cool pale ground like vellum under glass. The most "Medieval Europe" of the set and the highest contrast, which reads well on school laptops. The red is a true scarlet rather than an orange-red.

D · Terracotta and black. Athenian pottery

From black-figure and red-figure vases, the pottery of the agora itself. Near-black ink instead of navy, a burnt terracotta accent, a clay-coloured ground. Ties the brand to its name most directly and looks the least like other school sites. Riskier, because the near-black is heavier and the warmth can tip into brown if overused.
