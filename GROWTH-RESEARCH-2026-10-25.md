# Agorian: from one class to something bigger

## 1. Curriculum alignment

**Victorian Curriculum F–10 Version 2.0 (VCAA).** History is organised into two strands, Historical Knowledge (also labelled "Historical Knowledge and Understanding" on the V2.0 site) and Historical Concepts and Skills, with the skills strand built around sub-strands of chronology, historical sources as evidence, continuity and change, cause and effect, and historical significance; achievement standards sit at Levels 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10, so Year 8 is assessed against the Level 8 standard ([VCAA – History structure](https://victoriancurriculum.vcaa.vic.edu.au/the-humanities/history/introduction/structure); [VCAA F–10 V2.0 History curriculum](https://f10.vcaa.vic.edu.au/learning-areas/humanities/history/curriculum?view=full)). In V2.0, Levels 7–8 comprise overviews ("Asia and the Pacific Region c.600–1750" and "Europe and the Modern World c.600–1750") plus investigations; Medieval Europe is an option within Europe and the Modern World, and Japan under the Shoguns is an option within Asia and the Pacific Region alongside Angkor/Khmer, Mongol expansion and Polynesian expansion ([HTAV summary of History V2.0](https://www.htav.asn.au/documents/item/4465)). The Level 8 achievement standard opens "By the end of Level 8, students can analyse different interpretations of the establishment of early societies…" ([VCAA](https://f10.vcaa.vic.edu.au/learning-areas/humanities/history/curriculum?view=full)).

Code format is `VC2` + `HH` (History) + level + `K` (knowledge) or `S` (skills) + two digits, e.g. **VC2HH8K19** ("significant social, cultural, economic, environmental and political continuity and change…") and **VC2HH8S08** ("explain the causes and consequences of significant events, individuals, ideas and developments…") ([State Library Victoria curriculum documentation](https://www.slv.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-Medieval-Calligraphy_Curriculum-Documentation.pdf)). I could not retrieve the full text of the Japan- and Medieval-Europe-specific descriptions because the VCAA page loads them dynamically and the .docx downloads were unreadable from here; the authoritative list is the Levels 7–10 document ([VCAA History Levels 7–10](https://f10.vcaa.vic.edu.au/sites/default/files/2024-10/History%20Levels%207%E2%80%9310.docx)). Note that V2.0 is being phased in sector by sector and independent schools set their own timelines ([VCAA V2.0 overview](https://vcaa.vic.edu.au/curriculum/foundation-10-version-2/victorian-curriculum-f-10-version-20)).

**Australian Curriculum v9 (ACARA).** Codes run `AC9` + `HH` + year + `K`/`S` + two digits. Japan under the Shoguns sits under the Asia–Pacific world: AC9HH8K12–K16 (features of groups; a significant development or turning point; rulers and subject peoples; a significant individual or group; interpretations) ([NLA Digital Classroom](https://www.library.gov.au/learn/digital-classroom/japan-under-shoguns-794-1867)). Medieval Europe maps to AC9HH8K01–K06 and the skills to AC9HH8S01–S08 ([QCAA Year 8 History plan](https://www.qcaa.qld.edu.au/downloads/aciqv9/humanities-and-social-sciences/planning/ac9_hass_history_yr8_cap_example.pdf)).

**Machine-readable sources.** ACARA publishes v9 as RDF/XML, JSON-LD and SPARQL via the Machine-Readable Australian Curriculum, last updated 7 June 2024 ([ACARA MRAC](https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/machine-readable-australian-curriculum); [mrac.scootle.edu.au](https://mrac.scootle.edu.au/)). I could not find a stated licence on the MRAC pages; check ACARA's terms before redistributing. VCAA offers no API; the F–10 V2.0 site lets you download curriculum as CSV, DOCX or PDF ([VCAA download curriculum](https://f10.vcaa.vic.edu.au/download-curriculum)). For a static site, the practical path is a one-off CSV export turned into a JSON lookup of code, text and strand, then a `codes: []` field on each of the ~180 items. Most questions will carry one K code and one S code (e.g. a sakoku cause-and-effect item: VC2HH8K-something + VC2HH8S08, with AC9HH8K13 + AC9HH8S05 as the national equivalents). Expect a weekend's work: an hour on the data, the rest on tagging judgement.

## 2. Precedent

- **Education Perfect** began as "Language Perfect", a prototype Craig Smith built in high school; founded 2007 in Dunedin by brothers Shane and Craig Smith, renamed in 2014, and by 2020 served over 1 million students in 2,500+ schools, later gaining ST4S qualification in 2024 ([EP timeline](https://www.educationperfect.com/timeline/)). Current pricing is not published on that page.
- **Seneca Learning** (UK) built its first version in 2017, tested it in a 1,120-student trial, and reached 4 million UK users by 2020 ([The Seneca story](https://senecalearning.com/en-GB/blog/the-seneca-story/)). It remains free to sign up, with teachers able to set assignments on the free tier; revenue comes from Premium subscriptions sold to students, parents and schools ([Seneca help centre](https://help.senecalearning.com/en/articles/2483295-do-i-need-to-pay-how-does-seneca-make-money)).
- **Carousel Learning** (UK) grew from Adam Boxer's 2017 "Retrieval Roulette" spreadsheet, shared through the CogSciSci teacher community; a 2020 Innovate UK grant turned it into Carousel ([Carousel – about](https://carousel-learning.com/about)). Pricing: Silver free for individual teachers, Gold £70, Platinum quote-based for schools and trusts ([Carousel pricing](https://www.carousel-learning.com/learning)). This is the closest model to Agorian: a teacher's quizzing tool that stayed free for individuals and charged schools.
- **Edrolo** (Melbourne) started with curriculum-specific video tutorials "carefully matched to the final year curriculum of Victoria and NSW", joined Startmate in 2013, and hit its inflection point when it added textbooks and formative assessment; it now reports 250,000+ students in 1,100+ schools ([Blackbird investment notes](https://blackbird.vc/blog/investment-notes-edrolo)). Its store lists $150 Success Kits and $400 Years 7–10 Maths bundles ([Edrolo pricing](https://shop.edrolo.com.au/pages/pricing)).

Common pattern: start narrow and curriculum-specific, let teachers in free, charge institutions later, and treat ST4S-style assessment as the price of school procurement.

## 3. The hard parts

**Victorian government schools.** Before using a non-panel online tool with students, schools must check the Arc catalogue for a current ST4S report; if none exists they must submit a security impact assessment and complete a Privacy Impact Assessment before use, and may not use products rated Non-Compliant, Non-Participating or High Risk ([DE Policy and Advisory Library – ICT software risk assessment](https://www2.education.vic.gov.au/pal/ict-software-schools-risk-assessment/policy)). ST4S is the national ESA-run assessment of privacy, security and safety of education apps ([ESA – ST4S](https://esa.edu.au/solutions/our-solutions/safer-technologies-4-schools)). Government schools operate under the Privacy and Data Protection Act 2014 (Vic) and Health Records Act 2001 ([Schools' privacy policy](https://www.vic.gov.au/schools-privacy-policy)). I did not find a page on PROTECT specific to software vetting. Realistically, a sole trader with a Google Sheet log will not clear this bar without changing architecture; the simplest mitigation is to store no student names at all (class codes plus teacher-chosen aliases).

**Catholic and independent schools.** MACS is bound by the APPs under the Privacy Act 1988, conducts due diligence on third-party and cloud providers, and prefers Australian hosting or APP-equivalent jurisdictions ([MACS Privacy Policy](https://www.stjamesbrighton.catholic.edu.au/uploads/MACS-Privacy-Policy.pdf)). Independent schools are APP entities too, so contracts tend to push APP-equivalent obligations onto vendors.

**APPs and a sole trader.** A business with turnover of $3 million or less is generally outside the Privacy Act unless it trades in personal information or falls into another listed category, but it can opt in ([OAIC – Small business](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-for-organisations/small-business)). Schools will nonetheless expect APP-style handling; opting in and publishing a privacy policy is the credible route.

**ABN and .com.au.** An ABN requires carrying on or starting an enterprise; employee work does not qualify ([ABR – ABN entitlement](https://www.abr.gov.au/business-super-funds-charities/applying-abn/abn-entitlement)). Since 12 April 2021, .au licences require an Australian presence (citizenship, ABN, company or an Australian trade mark), and a trade mark only supports an exact-match domain ([Bird & Bird on auDA rules](https://twobirds.com/en/insights/2021/australia/auda-introduces-stricter-licensing-rules-for-au-domain-names)). With an ABN or the Agorian mark, agorian.com.au is registrable if nobody holds it; I did not check availability.

**IP with the employer.** Copyright in material made in the course of employment usually belongs to the employer; at independent schools this is the school unless agreed otherwise, with the Smartcopying guidance noting that a teacher's own textbook on their subject is theirs "unless the school had required" it ([Smartcopying – who owns copyright](https://smartcopying.edu.au/guidelines/copyright-basics/who-owns-copyright/)). Built on his own time and infrastructure, Agorian is probably his, but the contract governs; a short written acknowledgement from the school before commercialising is worth getting. This is general information, not legal advice.

**Accessibility.** Victorian Government online services must meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA, grounded in the Disability Discrimination Act ([Single Digital Presence](https://www.singledigitalpresence.vic.gov.au/node/455)); expect schools to ask for the same.

## 4. Growth paths

**(a) Free, open, tagged, with teacher sign-up.** Effort: a few weekends. Cost: hosting plus roughly $100–200 a year for domains; a lightweight auth layer if sign-up is more than a mailing list. Must be true: he wants reach, not income, and is content to carry the privacy burden by storing minimal data. First step: tag every item with VC2 and AC9 codes and publish a "curriculum map" page; post it to HTAV's community.

**(b) Paid per-class or per-school, Years 7–10 History.** Effort: a year of evenings to author three more year levels, plus ABN, privacy policy, ST4S assessment, WCAG audit and invoicing. Cost: several thousand dollars at least (ST4S fee not published; an accessibility audit; proper hosting). Must be true: ten or more teachers at other schools already use (a) unprompted and say they would pay. First step: pilot with three outside schools in 2027 Term 1 and ask what their business manager would need to sign.

**(c) Authoring platform.** Effort: a different product (editor, moderation, accounts, data per school). Must be true: (b) has paying schools and the bottleneck is his content, not theirs. First step: not yet; Carousel's path shows this comes years in.

## If I were you

Before the November exam, do nothing but keep the site working for your class; its value right now is your students' results.
Over the summer, spend one weekend on curriculum tagging and a public map page; it costs nothing and makes every later option possible.
Register the ABN and agorian.com.au only when a second school asks to use it.
Strip student names from the log before any outside class touches it; that single change removes most of the procurement friction.
Decide about paid growth in mid-2027 on evidence of outside use, not on how good the site feels today.
